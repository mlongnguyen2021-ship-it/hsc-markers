# Marking principles

## Evidence priority

Use the first available source in this order:

1. Exact question and same-year NESA marking criteria.
2. Same-year NESA sample answer as evidence of sufficient depth.
3. Relevant Biology Stage 6 syllabus outcome and content dot point.
4. Supplied Year 12 notes for definitions, examples, and acceptable depth.
5. Closely analogous NESA questions and criteria from other years.
6. General HSC judgement, clearly labelled as an estimate.

If sources conflict, prefer the exact official question and guide. Treat notes as supporting material, not as authority over NESA.

## What full marks mean

Award full marks when the response satisfies the top published criterion with accurate Year 12 biology. Do not require every sentence in the sample answer. A response can be shorter, use a different example, or use equivalent terminology and still receive full marks.

The sample answers in the supplied guides are often intentionally economical. Use their depth as a ceiling against overmarking: extra molecular, biochemical, medical, or statistical detail is not required unless the question or syllabus calls for it.

## Definitions and keywords

Expect brief definitions when a concept is central to the question or introduced as the basis of the response. A useful definition states the meaning and essential biological quality in one clause or sentence.

Require syllabus-specific terms when they carry the scientific distinction being tested. Examples include `allele`, `genotype`, `phenotype`, `mutation`, `pathogen`, `antigen`, `antibody`, `incidence`, `prevalence`, `reliability`, `validity`, and `accuracy` when relevant. Accept a correct definition expressed without the canonical word unless the task explicitly asks students to name or define the term.

Do not turn every noun into a definition requirement. Balance the user's preference for early definitions against the mark value and verb.

## Indicative depth when no exact guide exists

This scale is a cautious calibration, not an automatic formula:

- **1 mark:** One accurate and relevant identification, fact, result, or conclusion.
- **2 marks:** Two required elements, or one developed element with the relationship demanded by the verb.
- **3–4 marks:** Several accurate elements with at least one developed link, mechanism, comparison, interpretation, or application. Address the stimulus when present.
- **5–6 marks:** A coherent response covering the major components and relationships, using suitable biological terminology and evidence or examples.
- **7+ marks:** A sustained, logically organised response that integrates the required concepts, evidence, implications, and judgement at syllabus depth.

Never award or remove marks by counting sentences. Match quality to the criterion.

## Common ceilings

Apply a ceiling only when the omission or error prevents the response from meeting the higher criterion:

- correct facts but no required cause-and-effect link for `explain`;
- components listed but not related for `analyse`;
- evidence given but no judgement for `assess` or `evaluate`;
- generic knowledge that does not use a supplied graph, data set, experiment, or case study when required;
- a central misconception that reverses the mechanism;
- an answer about the wrong named disease, technology, organism, or process;
- a calculation with an unsupported answer when method is required.

Do not cap for a harmless wording imperfection, an omitted nonessential example, or an alternative correct sequence.

## Working Scientifically

Keep these distinctions precise:

- **Reliability:** Consistency or repeatability of results. Improve with repeats or replicates, adequate sample size, consistent procedure, and averaging where appropriate.
- **Validity:** Whether the investigation tests the intended relationship. Improve by controlling relevant variables, changing only the independent variable, and measuring the appropriate dependent variable.
- **Accuracy:** Closeness to the accepted or true value. Improve with calibrated, suitable-resolution equipment, careful measurement, and reduced systematic error.

When a student names an improvement, require the explanation demanded by the verb. For `justify`, link the precaution or method to the risk or quality it addresses.

## Feedback calibration

Name what the response demonstrated before naming what is missing. State the smallest actionable change that reaches the next band. Keep the model answer near the minimum secure full-mark standard. Do not rewrite a two-mark answer as an essay.
