# Marking principles

## Evidence priority

Use the first available source in this order:

1. Exact question and same-year official marking criteria.
2. Same-year official sample answer as evidence of sufficient depth.
3. Relevant Business Studies Stage 6 syllabus outcome and content point.
4. Supplied Year 12 notes for definitions, examples, case studies, and acceptable depth.
5. Closely analogous official questions and criteria from other years.
6. General HSC judgement, clearly labelled as an estimate.

If sources conflict, prefer the exact official question and guide. Notes are supporting evidence, not authority over NESA or BOSTES material.

## What full marks mean

Award full marks when the response satisfies the top published criterion with accurate Year 12 Business Studies knowledge. Do not require every sentence or example in a sample answer. Equivalent terminology, another relevant example, or a shorter answer can still earn full marks.

Use the sample answers to calibrate sufficient depth. Extra economic theory, accounting detail, management theory, or case-study history is not required unless the question or syllabus calls for it.

## Definitions and terminology

Expect a brief definition when a concept is central to the question or establishes the response. A useful definition states the meaning and essential business quality in one clause or sentence.

Require syllabus terminology when it carries the distinction being tested. Examples include `cost leadership`, `product differentiation`, `market segmentation`, `liquidity`, `gearing`, `working capital`, `outsourcing`, `global sourcing`, `industrial action`, and `corporate social responsibility` when relevant. Accept an accurate definition in equivalent words unless the task asks students to identify or define the named term.

Do not turn every noun into a definition requirement. Balance definitions against the command verb and marks available.

## Indicative depth without an exact guide

This is a cautious calibration, not an automatic formula:

- **1 mark:** One accurate, relevant identification, fact, feature, result, or conclusion.
- **2 marks:** Two required elements, or one developed element with the relationship demanded by the verb.
- **3–4 marks:** Several accurate elements with a developed relationship, explanation, comparison, interpretation, calculation, or application. Use the stimulus when required.
- **5–6 marks:** A coherent response covering the major concepts and relationships with relevant business terminology and evidence or examples.
- **7–10 marks:** A developed, logically organised response that addresses the required concepts, application, implications, and judgement at syllabus depth.
- **11–20 marks:** Use the published or analogous range descriptors. Higher ranges normally require extensive relevant knowledge, strong analysis or judgement, effective case-study or stimulus application when requested, business terminology, and sustained logical communication.

Never award or remove marks by counting paragraphs, definitions, examples, or case-study names. Match the response quality to the criterion.

## Common ceilings

Apply a ceiling only when the omission or error prevents the higher criterion from being met:

- correct concepts but no required cause-and-effect link for `explain`;
- components listed but not related for `analyse`;
- evidence given but no supported judgement for `assess` or `evaluate`;
- a generic answer that does not use a supplied stimulus, business, case study, or contemporary issue when required;
- a business name inserted without explaining how its evidence supports the concept;
- a central misconception that reverses the business effect or relationship;
- an answer about the wrong business function, strategy, influence, process, or indicator;
- a calculation with the wrong formula, missing method when required, or no interpretation when the question asks for one;
- an extended response that never addresses a major part of the question.

Do not cap for a harmless wording issue, a missing nonessential example, or an alternative valid structure.

## Business reports

When the question requests a business report, use the exact published criterion. Strong reports usually have clear report features, relevant headings, logical organisation, accurate terminology, direct use of the stimulus, and recommendations or judgement where the question requires them.

Do not require one fixed template. An executive summary, introduction, findings headings, conclusion, or recommendations can support communication, but content and application remain central. A polished report cannot compensate for missing business knowledge.

## Case studies and contemporary issues

Distinguish these levels:

- **Identifies:** Names a business or issue.
- **Refers to:** Adds a relevant fact or general example.
- **Uses:** Connects evidence to a business concept.
- **Applies or integrates:** Weaves specific evidence through the reasoning to show how or why it supports the analysis or judgement.

Only require this evidence when the question or criterion calls for it. Do not demand a specific business. Treat facts from the supplied notes cautiously when they may be time-sensitive, and do not invent missing details.

## Calculations

Use the formula expected by the syllabus or exact guide. Credit working when the guide allows it. Require currency symbols, percentage signs, ratios, units, and sensible rounding where relevant. Require interpretation only when the command verb or question asks the student to explain, analyse, assess, or evaluate the result.

## Feedback calibration

Name what the response demonstrated before naming what is missing. State the smallest actionable change that reaches the next range or full marks. Keep the model answer near the minimum secure full-mark standard; do not rewrite a two-mark answer as an essay.
