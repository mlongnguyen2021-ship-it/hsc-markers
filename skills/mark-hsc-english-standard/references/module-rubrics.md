## Common Module: Texts and Human Experiences

Focus on how texts represent individual and collective experiences, human qualities, emotions, behaviour, and motivations. Relevant ideas can include anomalies, paradoxes, and inconsistencies; challenges to assumptions; new ideas; personal reflection; storytelling; lives and cultures; and connections between the text, the responder, and the wider world.

Judge how well the response explains the ways form, mode, medium, context, purpose, structure, stylistic features, grammatical features, and language shape representation and meaning. Do not require every rubric phrase in every answer. Select only the concepts made relevant by the question and text.

## Module A: Language, Identity and Culture

Focus on how language reflects and shapes individual or collective identity and cultural perspectives. Relevant analysis can examine how texts affirm, ignore, reveal, challenge, or disrupt assumptions and beliefs, and how forms, conventions, structures, and language features communicate values and attitudes that influence perceptions.

Reward a clear, cohesive argument and specific analysis of the prescribed text. Do not use the supplied Advanced textual-conversation exemplar as Standard Module A content.

## Module B: Close Study of Literature

Focus on informed understanding and appreciation of a substantial prescribed text as a whole. Judge the student's interpretation of the text's ideas, people, settings, situations, distinctive qualities, language, and form. Strong responses analyse the interplay of ideas, form, and language and make evidence-based judgements about significance and meaning.

Personal response is valuable when it is informed by detailed textual knowledge. Do not reward unsupported personal opinion as analysis.

## Module C: The Craft of Writing

Focus on deliberate writing for authentic audience, purpose, context, and form. Relevant craft can include imagery, rhetoric, voice, characterisation, point of view, dialogue, tone, syntax, structure, motif, and control of conventions. Judge how choices convey ideas, evoke emotion, shape perspective, or share a vision.

For reflection, reward a specific account of the recursive writing process and the influence of prescribed or mentor texts when the question asks for it. A device name alone is insufficient.

## Supplied 2024 Paper 2 ranges

The user supplied a transcription of the 2024 HSC English Standard Paper 2 criteria. Use the exact question if available; the summaries below calibrate the ranges without making exemplar wording mandatory.

### Module A — 20 marks

- **17–20:** Highly effective explanation of how the prescribed text informs the question's term and its connection to culture; relevant, detailed references; effective organisation, development, and expression.
- **13–16:** Effective explanation and relevant references; competent organisation, development, and expression.
- **9–12:** Competent explanation with adequate references; adequate organisation and expression.
- **5–8:** Describes aspects of the text with some question relevance; limited references and organisation.
- **1–4:** Attempts to describe the text and organise an elementary response.

### Module B — 20 marks

- **17–20:** Highly effective evaluation of the extent to which the text reflects the supplied statement; relevant, detailed references; effective organisation, development, and expression.
- **13–16:** Effective evaluation with relevant references and competent control.
- **9–12:** Competent explanation of the extent, adequate references, and adequate organisation and expression.
- **5–8:** Description with some reference to the statement and text; limited organisation.
- **1–4:** Elementary attempt to describe the text and organise a response.

The shift from `evaluates` in higher ranges to `explains` in the 9–12 range is material. A response without a supported extent judgement should not enter the higher ranges merely because its textual knowledge is strong.

### Module C part (a) — 15 marks

- **13–15:** Engaging imaginative writing that effectively explores at least one stimulus idea; effective language craft; effective control of language and structure for audience, purpose, context, and form.
- **10–12:** Competent exploration, craft, and control.
- **7–9:** Sound exploration and adequate language with sound control.
- **4–6:** Attempts to engage with the stimulus and shows variable control.
- **1–3:** Attempts a piece with some relevance to the question.

### Module C part (b) — 5 marks

- **5:** Effectively explains one stylistic device in part (a) and how one Module C prescribed text inspired the composition, using relevant references and effective language control.
- **4:** Competent explanation of both elements with relevant references and competent control.
- **3:** Explains both elements with some references and sound control.
- **2:** Attempts to explain both the device and mentor-text influence.
- **1:** Attempts to respond.

Mark parts (a) and (b) separately before totalling them. Do not let a strong reflection retroactively raise the craft mark when the composition itself does not meet that descriptor.
