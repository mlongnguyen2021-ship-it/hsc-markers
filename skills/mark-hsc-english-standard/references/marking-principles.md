# Marking principles

## Holistic judgement

Match the response to the complete published descriptor, then choose the mark within that range. Do not convert a holistic English criterion into a mechanical checklist or award one mark per quotation, technique, paragraph, module word, or grammar correction.

Use the student's strongest sustained level, while accounting for omissions or contradictions that materially prevent the higher descriptor. One strong paragraph does not automatically lift an otherwise limited response into the top range; one awkward sentence does not erase a sustained effective response.

## Evidence priority

1. Exact question, stimulus, marks, and same-year official criteria.
2. Relevant official English Standard marking criteria supplied or transcribed by the user.
3. Relevant English Standard module rubric.
4. Official English Standard paper and response-booklet expectations.
5. English Standard practice questions and notes.
6. Advanced Common Module material for unseen-text practice only.
7. General HSC judgement, labelled as an estimate.

Do not use an Advanced essay, paper, or writing booklet to raise English Standard depth requirements. The supplied Plath and Hughes textual-conversation exemplar is Advanced content and is not a Standard Module A content model.

## Selecting a mark within a range

- **Top of range:** Fulfils all descriptor components in a sustained, convincing way, with only minor lapses.
- **Middle of range:** Clearly fulfils the descriptor, but quality or consistency varies.
- **Bottom of range:** Just meets the descriptor; weaknesses are substantial but do not place the response in the range below.

Explain the exact boundary. For example, distinguish an effective 15/20 from a highly effective 17/20 by identifying whether explanation, textual detail, and organisation are consistently effective rather than merely competent.

## Analytical responses

Strong analysis connects:

`question idea → textual detail → language/form choice → effect or meaning → module-relevant significance`

Technique identification without an explained effect is description. An effect that remains generic, such as “engages the reader,” is weak unless the response explains what the reader understands, feels, questions, or reconsiders and why that matters to the question.

Accept accurate paraphrase when exact quotation is unnecessary or unavailable. Reward integrated quotations when they support analysis, but do not require a fixed number. Context is useful only when it sharpens interpretation of the text and question.

## Short-answer calibration

- **1–2 marks:** A direct, accurate answer with the minimum evidence or explanation required.
- **3–4 marks:** A clear response using relevant textual detail and an explained connection to the question; more than one developed point when needed.
- **5–7 marks:** A developed, coherent analysis or comparison using well-selected evidence, explicit relationships, and a sustained response to the question.

These are guides only. Use exact criteria when available. Do not demand an introduction or conclusion for a short answer.

## Twenty-mark analytical responses

Consider the published descriptor's three recurring dimensions:

1. Quality of explanation, analysis, or evaluation in response to the exact question.
2. Relevance and detail of references to the prescribed text.
3. Organisation, development, and expression for audience, purpose, and form.

Top-range work normally sustains a defensible thesis, develops rather than repeats ideas, analyses the interaction of language or form with meaning, and uses detailed textual evidence. It need not imitate the vocabulary or conceptual density of an Advanced exemplar.

## Craft of Writing

For imaginative, discursive, persuasive, or informative work, judge the writing as the requested form rather than as an essay. Check whether the stimulus is meaningfully explored, not merely mentioned. Reward deliberate control of voice, imagery, rhetoric, characterisation, point of view, dialogue, tone, syntax, structure, and motif when they serve the idea and purpose.

Do not reward ornament for its own sake. A complex vocabulary error can weaken control more than a precise ordinary word. For reflective components, require explanation of the student's actual choices with short references to their composition and the nominated mentor text when specified.

## Grammar and expression

Quote the student's wording accurately, offer a corrected version, and name the issue briefly. Prioritise errors that affect meaning or recur:

- sentence boundaries, comma splices, fragments, and run-ons;
- subject–verb agreement and tense consistency;
- pronoun reference and modifier placement;
- apostrophes, quotation punctuation, and possessives;
- word choice, repetition, verbosity, and unclear abstraction;
- integration of quotations and titles;
- paragraph cohesion and transitions.

Do not rewrite the student's entire voice into inflated academic prose. Explain which corrections affect the mark and which are polish.

## Feedback depth

For complete 15–20 mark responses, provide at least 1000 words unless the user requests otherwise. Ground the judgement in multiple short excerpts from the student's work. Include newly written exemplar sentences targeted to the student's argument or craft, not generic templates and not copied source sentences.

For short answers, remain proportional. The feedback should normally be shorter than the student's revision task unless the user asks for a full lesson.

## Common ceilings

Use a ceiling only when a missing quality prevents the higher descriptor:

- a memorised response that does not answer the question;
- description of plot or content without analysis required by the verb;
- technique listing with generic or absent effects;
- assertion of a module concept without textual demonstration;
- limited or inaccurate reference to the prescribed text;
- no evaluative judgement when the question asks “to what extent” or uses `evaluate`;
- comparison that discusses texts separately without establishing a relationship;
- Module C writing that barely connects to the stimulus or loses control of form;
- reflection that names a device or mentor text without explaining its influence;
- persistent language errors that impede meaning or control.

Do not cap for an unconventional but defensible interpretation, a different paragraph structure, or the absence of a particular technique or quotation from an exemplar.
