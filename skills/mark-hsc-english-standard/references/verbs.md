# HSC question verbs

Use the verb to determine the thinking the response must demonstrate. A student does not need to repeat the verb.

- **Analyse:** Identify components and the relationships between them; draw out and relate implications.
- **Assess:** Make a judgement of value, quality, outcomes, results, or size.
- **Compare:** Show how things are similar or different.
- **Compose:** Create a text for the specified audience, purpose, context, and form.
- **Contrast:** Show how things are different or opposite.
- **Critically analyse:** Interpret and reason across evidence to make a judgement based on detailed analysis.
- **Critically evaluate:** Evaluate with accuracy, knowledge, logic, questioning, reflection, and quality.
- **Define:** State meaning and identify essential qualities.
- **Describe:** Provide characteristics and features.
- **Discuss:** Identify issues and provide points for and/or against.
- **Evaluate:** Make a judgement based on criteria; determine value.
- **Explain:** Relate cause and effect, make relationships evident, or provide how and why.
- **Identify:** Recognise and name.
- **Interpret:** Draw meaning from.
- **Justify:** Support an argument or conclusion.
- **Outline:** Indicate the main features in general terms.
- **Reflect:** Consider choices or experiences and explain their significance, often with evidence from the student's own composition and studied texts.
- **To what extent:** Make and sustain a qualified judgement about degree.

## Practical interpretation

- For **identify**, do not demand explanation unless another part requires it.
- For **describe** or **outline**, credit accurate features without forcing causal analysis.
- For **explain**, require a clear how, why, relationship, or effect.
- For **analyse**, require a relationship between textual choice, effect or meaning, and the question; technique spotting is insufficient.
- For **compare**, require an explicit relationship. Separate mini-analyses without a comparative link do not fully satisfy the verb.
- For **assess**, **evaluate**, **justify**, or **to what extent**, require evidence and a supported judgement.
- For **discuss**, require a developed consideration of the statement or issues, not a list of both sides.
- For **compose**, judge how deliberately the writing fulfils the requested idea, audience, purpose, context, and form.
- For **reflect**, require specific explanation of choices rather than a summary of what was written.
