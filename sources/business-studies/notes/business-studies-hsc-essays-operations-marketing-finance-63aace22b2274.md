Analyse the operations strategies used to help sustain a competitive advantage. (20 marks)

In a dynamic business environment, operational management strategies are critically used to help
sustain and achieve a competitive advantage. Specifically, leading-edge technology is
implemented and used to help businesses obtain a place and status in the market. Quality
management, namely quality control, is enforced to improve standards and achieve greater
quality products compared to competitors. Finally, businesses utilising the strategy of new
product or service design and development will be able to enhance and sustain a competitive
advantage in the market.  Prominent businesses like McDonalds, Apple, Woodman’s Markets,
Keytone Dairy and Muratbey have tailored their use of such operations strategies to achieve and
sustain their competitive advantage in the market in a post-Covid-19 environment.

Leading-edge technology

Technology refers to the scientific knowledge and equipment available to help a business
perform certain functions. Technology may be defined as the design, construction and/or
application of innovative devices, methods and machinery upon operations processes. A business
can hence obtain sustainable competitive advantage through use of technology as through it,
there is better quality and speed (reduced lead times to the consumer). There is a strong
correlation between utilising leading-edge technology and sustaining a competitive advantage.
Leading-edge technology is technology that has never been used or introduced in the market.
Businesses who use this are the first to develop and implement the new technology.

Specifically, a positive implication of the implication of leading-edge technology is that it can
improve efficiency of the production process, increased efficiency of the production and
decrease overall lead times. McDonald’s efficiently has recently improved after acquiring Drive-
Thru technology in response to the covid-19 pandemic. The recent acquisition of dynamic yield
(a technology company) in 2019 enabled them to develop a sophisticated technology that adapts
the menu board according to a range of factors including weather and previous orders. This
technology enabled McD to amplify their suggestive selling efforts since 90% of sales were
processed in the drive-thru of most stores. Notably, it decreased drive-thru time for each person
from 253 seconds to 208 seconds. In effect, this allowed for more customers to be satisfied with
the McD service in a time of heavy congestion in fast food restaurants. The overall lead times
was reduced and improved the service dramatically which altogether gave McD a competitive
advantage in the midst of covid-19 through the use of leading-edge technology as an operations
strategy.

Furthermore, another positive implication is seen through Woodman’s markets. During Covid-
19, they had to keep up with the demand of heavy stock as people were buying more groceries
with closures of many restaurants and home isolation. They had to scan and monitor stock for
approx. 100 000 items. Here, they decided to invest in leading-edge technology from badger
technologies robots with badger retail insight. Effectively, this increased the speed and quality
for woodman’s markets as they were able to scan items in a matter of hours rather than days.
They identified items no longer in stock with 95% precision and identification of mislabelled

items with 90% precision. This leading-edge tech was hence able to give woodman’s a
competitive advantage as more customers were satisfied with the quality and lead times they
offered, having a product and service that was more appealing than competitors in their eyes and
generating more sales.

However, a negative implication is seen within McD’s implementation of their leading-edge
technology; dynamic yield through the high up-front cost with almost $480 million. Moreover, it
may hinder Woodsman’s speed in the short term as the business may need to temporarily close in
order to implement this technology, retrain staff and change process layout.

Thus, there is a strong relationship between the operations strategy of leading-edge tech and
sustaining a competitive advantage in the market.

Quality control

competitive advantage can be successfully sustained through the implementation of the quality
control strategy. Quality refers to the way a product is produced in relation to the process
conducted, and quality control assists with this as it involves the physical inspections of the
product in all stages of the production process. There is a strong correlation between the
implementation of quality control as an operations strategy and the achievement of a competitive
advantage. The advantage over others is seen through the enhanced quality.

A positive implication of implementing quality control is seen through McD and their burger
buns. It is an effective operations strategy that allows inspections of the product in  all stages of
the production process including feed forward(before), concurrent(during) and feedback(after)
controls. The New Zealand business ARYTZA Bakery supplies McD with high quality buns.
This bakery undergoes quality control through maintaining the quality of buns that are consistent
in taste, size, tone and appearance. A worker physically checks and maintains the quality of the
buns and their appearance throughout every stage of the production process. Namely, the eye-pro
Inspection system is what identities the variations in appearance of the buns such as tine, height
and heaviness. This strategy successfully allowed McD to maintain their quality and meet
customer quality expectations and standards. This will ultimately increase customer satisfaction
through the achievement of reliability and ultimately sustain and achieve a competitive
advantage within the market.

However, a negative implication of the association between the operations strategy of quality
control and competitive advantage is seen through the increased expenses related to the staff
needing to inspect each item for defects. Another expensive consideration is that staff need to be
adequately trained to carry out these quality processes. Consequently, this will have a domino
effect resulting in decreased quality, customer satisfaction and overall sales. This has clearly
been reflected through Apple whereby 35% of iPhones sold through JB-HI-FI have been
recalled. The recall was due to deficiencies evident in the products as the staff were not
vigilantly conducted their duty of quality control practices. The lack of staff initiative had a
direct negative impact on the quality of the product and hence on customer standards and
expectations; ultimately affecting competitive advantage for JB-HI-FI in the market.

Thus, there is a strong relationship between the operations strategy of quality control and
sustaining a competitive advantage in the market.

The design, development, launch and sale of new products enables a business to grow and to
attain a competitive advantage. A business that has the ability to integrate leading-edge
technologies with skills and innovative ideas can be a market leader in new products and
maintain a competitive advantage/edge. There is a strong correlation between the implementation
of new product design and development as an operations strategy and the achievement of
competitive advantage, providing an innovative edge to businesses who use this strategy.

The positive implication of this association whereby new product design and development is
used as a strategy to achieve competitive advantage is seen through Keytone dairy. COVID-19
has caused a huge variation in demand worldwide. Consumers have heavily changed their
consumer habits since isolation and deciding to purchase healthier and immune boosting
products to avoid illnesses. Hence, Keytone dairy has launched a new immune boosting
supplement that will initially be available in Chinese markets; and hence was able to target
customers’ changing needs during the pandemic. They were able to hence achieve over
$11million in sales in one quarter of 2020; corresponding to 33% rise in sales from the previous
quarter, and were able to hence achieve and sustain competitive advantage through their use of
implementing and considering new consumer trends and demands to satisfy target markets.

Moreover, another positive implication of this relationship is seen through Muratbey; a Turkish
cheese company. Over two years it implemented research and development to launch a new
product aiming to gain competitive edge during COVID-19. They implemented a new product
range called ‘MuratBey Plus’ which includes cheese products with added benefit of Vitamin D.
as a result of their success, they increased sales domestically and began exporting to Europe.
These exports accounted for 25% revenue and amount exported increased by 40% since
launching this new product range.

However, a negative implication is seen through the increased expenses and time. Keytone dairy
and Muratbey successfully monitored the changes in demand and responded with the
development of new products which suited to many new target markets; ultimately increasing
costs and is a time-consuming process. Specifically, Muratbey took two years to create their new
product which came at the risk of failing.

Thus, there is a strong relationship between the operations strategy of new product design and
development and sustaining a competitive advantage in the market.

Conclusively, operations strategies are critically used in business environments to help sustain
competitive advantages and edge. Namely, strategies of leading-edge tech, quality control and
new product or design and development all work and strive for customer satisfaction and
efficiency in the business environment to generate sales and most significantly, achieve

competitive advantage. This is seen is a post-COVID-19 business environment in prominent
businesses such as McD, Apple, Ketone Dairy and Muratbey.

Analyse the internal and external sources of finance used to achieve the objectives of
financial management (20 marks)

Objectives of finance can successfully be achieved in a dynamic business environment through
the use of internal and external sources of finance. Specifically, the internal source of finance
such as retained profits can effectively be used for the achievement of solvency as a financial
objective. Secondly, external long-term debt; namely leasing can be used for the achievement of
liquidity as a financial objective. And lastly, external equity finance can be used to achieve the
financial objective of growth. Prominent businesses such as McDonald’s, Commonwealth Bank
of Australia (CBA), Peter Sheppard and Forbidden Foods tailored and benefited from the use of
these internal and external sources of finance to achieve their financial objectives in a post-
COVID-19 environment.

Retained profits
Internal sources of finance come from within the business and are recorded under equity in the
balance sheet. Retained profits is a form of internal source of finance and is known as
‘undistributed profits’. This is when a business makes profit, and reinvests this back into the
business. There is a strong correlation between the internal source of retained profits in finance

and the achievement of solvency as a financial objective which is the ability to pay off both
short-term and long-term business obligations and commitments.

The positive implication of using retained profits as an internal source of finance to achieve the
financial business objective of solvency is seen through the Commonwealth Bank of Australia
(CAD). They have doubled their funds set aside for bad loans (those that cannot be repaid)-$2.5
billion in 2020. In total, CBA provided $6.4 billion worth of bad/doubtful debts and noted $8.7
billion worth of troublesome loans. Despite reporting a 12% increase in net profit, CBA was
effectively able to retain approximately a quarter of its net profit to deal with the harsh
implications of COVID-19. This internal source of finance implemented by CBA allowed them
to rely less on debt finance which comes with more costs and expenses (interest repayments).
Moreover, they used and took advantage of  the retained profits in a timely manner to deal with
the financial implication COVID-19 posed and gave them a quick source of funds which was
used in emergency situations. This internal source of finance was successfully able to achieve the
financial objective of solvency as CBA was able to pay off their short-term commitments in a
timely manner, as well as reducing their extent of gearing in the long-term; making them more
financially stable.

Moreover, another positive implication of using retained profits as an internal source of finance
to achieve the financial objective of solvency is seen through McD. In 2019, they made a profit
of $6 billion and decided to keep it in the business. This allowed them to easily give more than
half of this profit as a form of dividends to shareholders and fund share buybacks as well as
using it to fund future programs without the use and risk of loans and debt financing; ultimately
achieving the financial objective of solvency as a result.

However, the negative implication of this relationship is that retained profits is a limited source
and its source is unpredictable depending on the amount of sales made. Hence, CBA and McD
can easily misuse this source of finance as it is not accountable to anyone (no interest).

Hence, there is a strong relationship between using retained profits as an internal source of
finance and achieving the financial objective of solvency.

Leasing
Leasing is an external source of finance and is also a form of long-term borrowing (debt). It is
the use of an asset (typically non-current asset) over an agreed period of time with ongoing
regular repayments. There is a strong correlation and relationship between using leasing as an
external source of finance and achieving the financial objective of liquidity which is the ability to
pay off short-term debts as they fall due.

The positive implication of this relationship is seen through Peter Sheppard. It is an iconic
footwear retailer that has stores across Australia. The business pays $123 000 every month in

lease repayments in return for operating a 1000-square metre shop at Pitt Mall in the Sydney
CBD. This allows Peter Sheppard to use the asset without a large capital outlay, instead the
business just makes lease payments to Westfield every month. Another advantage of using
leasing as an external source of finance is that lease payments are tax-deductible, thus reducing
the business’ taxable income by the total amount of lease payments made. These lease
repayments as a result retains more cash in the business so that it could be sued for other
purposes and fulfill short-term commitments Peter Sheppard must oblige to such as accounts
payable; ultimately achieving the financial objective of liquidity.
Another positive implication of using leasing as an external source of finance is seen through
McD. they have made the use of leasing as a source of funding; paying almost $1.6 billion in
lease payments worldwide. McD uses this strategy to reduce the initial capital outlay required for
new stores, enabling more stores to be open in a shorter timeframe. It also helps retain more cash
in the business as it can be used for multiple other purposes that McD can benefit from,
especially during the midst of COVID-19; being able to repay their short-term obligations and
debt and ultimately achieve the financial objective of liquidity.

However, a negative implication of using leasing as an external source of finance is that Peter
Sheppard and McD are not able to retain full ownership of the stores they rent which poses risk
once the owners decide to sell the asset; hence impacting on level of liquidity they are able to
reach to meet their short-term business obligations and financial commitments.

Equity
External equity finance refers to funds sourced from people and institutions who are external to
the business. Specifically, new share issues refer to the process of raising equity finance by
selling shares for the first time on the stock exchange. There is a strong relationship between
external equity and achieving the financial objective of growth.

The positive implication of this relationship is seen through Australian health food business
‘Forbidden Foods’ which floated on the ASX in August 2020. They were successfully able to
raise $6 million from a new share issue after releasing its shares at $0.20 each. Investors clearly
liked what the health food company was offering, with the stock doubling in less than 20 minutes
of it floating back on ASX. as a result, they were able to grow to supply over 3000 retailers, 500
restaurants and food manufacturers, exporting to customers in NZ, Ireland, Singapore and the
US. the use of this external source of finance; equity capital allowed Forbidden Foods to launch
new innovative lines, deepen its market penetration and broaden its international focus;
ultimately achieving the financial objective of growth which is able to generate more profit and
sales for the Australian business.

However, a negative implication of this relationship is that through using external equity; namely
new issue of shares, ownership becomes diluted which means original owners have less
control/ownership of the business.

Hence, there is a strong relationship and correlation between the use of equity as an external
source of finance and achieving the financial objective of growth.

Conclusively, objectives of finance can successfully be achieved in a dynamic business
environment through the use of internal and external sources of finance. Namely, internal
sources of retained profits, and external sources of finances such as leasing and equity can be
used to achieve the financial objectives of solvency, liquidity and growth respectively. This is
evidently shown in prominent businesses such as the Commonwealth Bank of Australia,
McDonald’s, Peter Sheppard and Forbidden Foods in a post-COVID-19 environment.

Explain the influence of global markets on financial management of businesses. (20 marks)

The influence of global markets inevitably impacts on financial management and strategies of
businesses. The impact of globalisation has greatly changed and manipulated businesses
financial decisions. Specifically, the global market influence of economic outlook allows
businesses to engage in cost controls in order to overcome the global market influence, namely
through expense minimisation. The global market influence of availability of funds also impacts
a business’s financial management through the use of derivatives. Finally, interest rates heavily
impact financial management of businesses. The effectiveness and impact these global market
influences have on financial management is reflected through Qantas, McD and Apple. Thus,
global market influences greatly impact and influence a business’ decisions and implementations
of financial management strategies.

Economic outlook-Expense Minimisation
 Economic outlook refers specifically to the projected changes to the level of economic growth.
In a positive economic outlook, the effect is increased jobs, more money to spend, greater

demand for products and businesses do well. However, in a negative economic outlook the effect
is the opposite. The impact of a negative economic outlook is shown through the COVID-19
pandemic crisis. This resulted in many individuals losing their jobs thus providing them with less
disposable income which has a detrimental flow on effect towards businesses.

In 2020 the global pandemic caused rapid revenue declines creating challenges for Qantas.
Qantas responded by cutting flying capacity, deferring and cancelling orders for new planes,
restructuring and replacing Jetstar with some routes ultimately minimising Qantas's Fixed and
variable costs. Clearly economic outlook has caused this business to result in utilising the cost
control strategy decreasing fixed and variable costs in order to compensate for the losses. In
effect this allowed Qantas to survive the pandemic and ultimately minimise expenses. Evidently,
the negative economic outlook caused by the pandemic has resulted in the use of expense
minimisation to compensate for such losses. Additionally, Qantas saw the need to create a
COVID-19 recovery plan where by the end of 2023, they will aim to save $15 billion through
expense minimisation. Specifically, Qantas is on track to achieve this as they have made 20,000
employees redundant to date, saving on the large expense of wages.

Moreover, the COVID-19 pandemic also drastically impacted Apple. Since the start of the
pandemic, many countries have faced closures and temporary lockdowns. Specifically, many
countries in China could not operate due to restrictions. The economic outlook was therefore
negative and gas made an immense impact on the global tech giant, Apple. This negative
economic outlook caused the profit and sales of businesses to decrease; declaring that revenue
goal of $65 billion in 2020 will not be achieved for Apple due to the pandemic. In effect, Apple
overcame this global market influence challenge through closing down the operations of 42 of
their stores to ensure safety of all employees. This cost-cutting and expense minimisation
strategy cut down on wages, electricity and on store cost; ultimately shifting most of their sales
online. This allowed Apple to focus on their core and improve on their online platform in order
to still reach the revenue goal they had in mind.
Overall, the financial management strategy of expense minimisation assisted Qantas and Apple
to get through the global market influence of poor economic outlook.

Availability of funds + hedging
Availability of funds refers to the ease of access of funds and loans on the international market.
The availability of funds is important for the business to be aware of because financial managers
understand the risk of not having the ability to access funds as this will limit possible growth or
they may need funds for business activities. The influence of availability of funds is seen through
the ability of businesses to borrow globally if there is a lack of finance in their own country.
However, the availability of overseas funds is limited due to the transaction exposure.
Transaction exposure refers to the risk that exchange rates may change after the business has

already entered financial obligation. Hence this clearly limits the availability of funds because it
can cause the business to pay more.

The effect of exchange rate fluctuations limiting the access of funds is seen through Qantas who
benefit from hedging in order to insure itself against unfavourable economic conditions as a
majority of its operating lease payments and purchases of new planes are acquired through US
currency. Qantas makes effective use of forward contracts, locking in one currency for another at
an agreed rate in future for the company’s fuel, whereby they have hedged over 86% of its fuel
expenses in 2018.

This is significantly beneficial for the airline company as they are able to maximise
profitability and remain more competitive in the global market, whereby they have more
income for growth through purchasing new and improved aircraft.

Additionally, Qantas predicts domestic flight activity will be at 68 percent of pre-pandemic
levels by April and between 90 per cent and 100 per cent by July of 2022. Qantas is temporarily
insulated from soaring fuel costs thanks to its hedging program through to the end of June. As
fuel prices are currently at their peak due to the Ukraine/Russia dispute and impact, hedging has
affected Qantas advantageously as they have a fixed cost and will not further be impacted by
sudden increase in price. Availability of funds influences hedging for Qantas in order to improve
on their profitability and stay away from undesirable global factors.

Hence, hedging has ensured that the global market influence of availability of funds is stabilised
and able to be met in a current crisis of high prices for fuel, and relatively easier access to funds
internationally.

Interest rates
Interest rates refers to the cost of borrowing money. Businesses are now able to borrow funds
overseas as interest rates can be lower than the home country because of global interest rate
fluctuations. Since businesses are able to access global funds, this has provided the opportunity
for greater financing. Recently Qantas has taken advantage of low global interest rates resulting
in saving millions of dollars in interest repayments. However, the corresponding interest rate
offered by that country may create the need for businesses to respond to possible fluctuations
which can cause a detrimental impact. As global interest rates increase, businesses will not
borrow as much money as they did when the interest rates are lower. The effect of fluctuations in
interest rates causes great concerns for financial managers hence the response of financial
strategies.

This is evident through Qantas's use of fixing interest rates. This allowed Qantas to choose a rate
at a specific period of time in order to pay the interest resulting in no transaction exposure.

0Therefore, the global influence of interest rates and the possible fluctuations that come with it
has resulted in businesses implementing a strategy such as fixing the rate to overcome any
negative financial effects.

McDonald’s is a fast-food company which borrows funds from overseas and therefore, may
experience fluctuations in variable interest rates. The company has engaged in international
borrowing as seen through 40% of its debt being sourced from global lenders. McDonald’s has
thereby made use of seeking cheaper sources of finance to reduce costs as they are relying more
on debt finance than equity, thus increasing their gearing.

Due to varying international interest rates, McDonald’s financial managers have made use of
global financial strategies by fixing these rates through the use of interest rate swaps.
McDonald’s was thereby able to fix roughly 90% of its total debt at an average interest rate of
3.3% p.a., which is a 0.2% reduction from 2016. In effect, the company is able to minimise the
financial risks of the business and reduce costs of high variable interest rates globally.

Conclusively, the influence of global markets inevitably impacts on financial management and
strategies of businesses and is critical for businesses to consider their impact in a dynamic
business environment. Economic outlook, availability of funds and interest rates impact the
financial strategies of expense minimisation and hedging to help dominate businesses in the
external global environment.

Global Customisation and Identifying target markets
The implementation of global customisation as a global marketing strategy is useful in aiding a
business to identify their target market. Customisation is a global marketing strategy that
involves businesses producing and developing a different product for each country or market in

order to meet their specific needs and demands by diversifying the product. A target market is a
group of consumers for whom a particular product had been developed. Businesses that are able
to identify their target market are able to create promotion materials that are more relevant and
are able to refine the marketing strategies they utilise to make the global strategy of
customisation more relevant. There is a strong correlation between using global customisation as
a global marketing strategy and identifying target markets.

Specifically, an advantage of global customisation is that a business is able to identify their
differing target markets around the world which is meticulously exhibited through McDonald’s.
Upon opening up in a new country McDonalds were able to customise their menu depending on
the country they operate in, by identifying their new target market and targeting them
accordingly which allows them to maximise their profits. This customisation is evident when
they introduced the all-vegetarian menu upon opening in India and including the McAloo wrap
within their menu to cater for their culture as well as the halal meat option when opening in
Muslim dominating countries. They have benefited from utilising this customisation strategy as
they were able to identify the differing target markets across the world and appeal to the hobbies
and traditions of particular countries such as Italy and Spain in Europe through the inclusion of
beer on its menu. For example, in India, the McAloo wrap is on the menu to cater for Buddhist
beliefs and demographics. Moreover, in Germany, beer is offered on the menu to cater to the
German culture. This is deemed advantageous to McDonalds as the different menu options cater
towards the differing target markets of consumers, hence making them more inclined to consider
the fast-food company. Therefore, not only does the business build a positive reputation amongst
consumers globally, but they were also able to increase their customer base, improving their
revenue margin dramatically to US$21 billion, achieving their profitability objective.

Nevertheless, Sephora is a global makeup brand which implemented global customisation
however failed to identify their target market’s needs which resulted in their product being
scratched of the shelves. Their implementation of customisation showed no success when they
attempted to introduce a plant and herb-based make up products in countries located in the
Middle East as they couldn’t identify their target market’s understanding towards these products.
Various language barriers prevented the business from reaching their target market to promote
the new makeup products catered towards their traditions. Consequently, Sephora was forced to
scratch the new products off the shelf, losing approximately $2 million on newly researched and
developed products which did not sell well in the Middle East.

Despite Sephora failing to customise products due to their inability to identify their target market
McDonalds are able to maximise their profitability and improve their customer satisfaction rate
to 90% through properly identify their target market’s needs and beliefs and changing their menu
accordingly, and exceeding the expectations of their global target market, valuing their culture
and traditions.
